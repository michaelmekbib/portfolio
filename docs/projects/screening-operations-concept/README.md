# One Passenger One Record

I designed an integrated screening-operations concept for 1CDP to simplify passenger data management during outbreak response at ports of entry.

**Status:** Innovation concept under review  
**Context:** 2026 Ebola response at Washington Dulles  
**Focus:** Workflow redesign, data integration, exception management, operational reporting

## The challenge

During response operations, staff reconciled manifests, spreadsheets, screening records, and schedules across separate tools. Repeated entry of passenger and flight information added handoffs, version conflicts, and reconciliation work.

## My contribution

I translated observations from the response into a reusable “enter once and reuse” operating model. The concept brings manifest import, worklists, assessments, exception review, reporting, and staffing into one connected workflow.

| Stage | Proposed capability |
| --- | --- |
| Import | Validate manifests and manage versions. |
| Assign | Generate port and flight worklists. |
| Screen | Pre-populate known passenger and flight details. |
| Resolve | Route duplicate, incomplete, inconsistent, and unmatched records for review. |
| Monitor | Connect screening status, referrals, exceptions, and staffing coverage. |

The design includes role-based views, configurable disease and location rules, deployer scheduling, and proposed QARS integration.

## Expected impact

The proposed capability is designed to shorten assessment preparation, reduce transcription corrections, improve referral visibility, and give response leaders a shared operational picture.

My evaluation plan focuses on time to a validated worklist, time to the first assessment question, automatic match rate, exception rate, and staffing visibility.

## Implementation approach

I proposed discovery and design, an MVP, a Washington Dulles pilot, and enterprise hardening. The initial estimate is **4–6 months**, dependent on architecture, integration, authorization, and roadmap decisions.
